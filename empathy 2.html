Empathy in Science Communication

In this course, learn how to empathize when talking about controversial science topics.

39 Cards

Niloo and Gerdoo are good friends, but sometimes they disagree… a lot of times about science! Niloo gets frustrated and angry when Gerdoo ignores scientific evidence.

Niloo could be more empathetic toward Gerdoo. But why be empathetic? Why put ourselves in other's shoes? Why can’t we just share facts and evidence?

When you really like someone, you don’t want to believe they might have done something bad. Similarly, when you have believed in something for a long time, you don’t want to accept that it might be wrong.

Changing our attitude about something or someone can be really hard, especially when that thing or person is connected to our beliefs, values or groups we belong to.

We might ignore evidence and facts to hold on to a belief. We might also find reason to criticize information that goes against our existing beliefs.

So you see, we need empathy and understanding to start a hard conversation. Facts are just not enough.

So, how can we bring our hearts closer together and be more empathetic?

Continue to learn more.

First, spend time getting to know others. Gerdoo is a bit scared of vaccines. They tell Niloo, "Why would I get a syringe filled with gross goo in it?? I would never do that."

It would be easy for Niloo to immediately assume that Gerdoo is just "anti-science" or "not sophisticated enough" to understand vaccine science.

But if Niloo listens… really listens to Gerdoo, they might learn more.

Niloo can ask Gerdoo why they feel the way they do, questions that help Niloo listen.

"What have you heard about the vaccine? What are you concerned about? And why is that concern important to you?"

Niloo can also pay attention to Gerdoo’s face, tone of voice and body language. To do so, Niloo needs to truly observe.

Maybe Gerdoo has heard about a friend who has vaccinated their child and the child experienced bad side-effects.

If Niloo really listens, they might hear in Gerdoo’s voice emotions of concern, worry, fear, and maybe anger.

Niloo might realize that they are not talking to an anti-science person, but rather to a concerned person who values their health, their safety and their community.

Niloo can’t help Gerdoo without knowing what’s going on in Gerdoo’s mind. Niloo can’t help Gerdoo by making fun of them and their emotions, judging them or throwing data at them.

Listen attentively and observe with patience. Niloo can know Gerdoo better by listening.

You can know your audiences through a conversation, focus group, interview or reading blogs or forums they've written.

Maybe even set goals accordingly: "In this conversation, I JUST want to get to know you, see where you are coming from and what you feel and think."

Put yourself in other’s shoes. Imagine what Gerdoo felt when they heard about a scary story of what vaccines did to someone...

Now imagine a time when you were scared about something that you didn’t know much about. Imagine how it would have felt for others to dismiss your concern.

It must be hard to feel worried, isolated and misunderstood.

Guess what? You just put yourself in the shoes of anyone else who's felt the same way!

Niloo is practicing this now. To relate to Gerdoo, they imagine how Gerdoo might feel and think.

Niloo is even learning new, unexpected things. "If I were Gerdoo, I would also be really scared. I would never trust someone who dismisses my feelings and fears."

"I would want someone to ask me what I want to know, and tell me what has been done to make sure vaccines are really safe. What about scary stories I’ve heard? What about those?"

It’s this emotional connection that can help Niloo respond to Gerdoo’s questions with more empathy.

But putting yourself in other's shoes takes practice. Try it!

Activity!

Next time you read about or interact with someone you disagree with, pause and ask yourself what is behind their words. Keep asking why.

Try to see the world through their eyes, with their concerns and beliefs. Why might you refuse to get the vaccine? Why might you be scared? Why might you not trust someone? Maybe it is a worry for the family, or not knowing the experts, or having have heards scary stories.

Now you are more prepared to reach out to them.

Respond with kindness and honesty. When Niloo heard Gerdoo out and put themselves in Gerdoo’s place, Niloo had a better understanding of "why" Gerdoo might reject scientific facts.

Niloo can let Gerdoo know that their feelings are real and there is no judgement about their feelings: "Yeah, I bet that must be really scary…"

Connect with others by letting them know what values you both share. Niloo might share a personal story about their own feelings and concerns. "I worry about our parents getting sick."

Be transparent and honest.

"Honestly, I don’t know much about this, but I’d be happy to ask around or send you some information from sources I trust."

Show integrity. Niloo is Gerdoo’s close friend. Niloo makes sure there is care in their words and their actions.

”You know how much I care for you, I just really want to make sure you’re safe.”

Remember, closer hearts can hear each other better and, maybe, help one another beat more in harmony. We just need to listen, share and respond with kindness.

Activity

Ok, it’s time to turn our assumptions into curiosity!

Think of someone you know. Start assuming things about them: ideas they believe in, ideas they refuse, the reason why they believe in certain ideas, etc.

Now, change all of your assumptions into questions: Do they actually believe in this? What makes them believe in that? Am I sure? What is the full picture?
